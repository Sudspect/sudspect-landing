How to Attract Pet Owners to Your Apartment Complex in 2026

Pet owners are the most loyal — and most overlooked — renter demographic in Florida. Here's how property managers can win them over before the competition does.

If you manage a residential community in Florida, you've probably noticed a shift in who's touring your units. More applicants are asking about pet policies before they even ask about rent. That's not a coincidence. According to a Multifamily Dive analysis, 59% of renters now have at least one pet — up from 46% in 2019 — and 60% say a property's pet policy is a significant reason they renew their lease.

For property managers and leasing directors, that's a signal worth paying attention to. The question isn't whether you should go pet-friendly. It's how to do it in a way that attracts quality residents, protects your property, and sets your community apart in a competitive market.

Understand What Pet Owners Actually Want

Pet owners aren't just looking for a building that tolerates their dog — they're looking for one that genuinely supports life with a pet. That distinction matters in your marketing copy and on your property.

The most-searched amenity on Zillow isn't in-unit washer/dryers or a rooftop terrace — it's pet friendliness. Zillow data shows that pet-friendly filters are toggled more than twice as often as any other amenity on the platform. And 44% of pet owners say they've passed on a property specifically because it didn't allow pets.

Before you invest in any amenities, survey your existing residents and your recent applicants. You'll likely find that the biggest asks fall into three buckets: outdoor access, on-site convenience, and community belonging. Knowing which of these your property currently lacks tells you exactly where to invest first.

Audit and Update Your Pet Policy

A pet policy full of blanket breed bans and sub-20-pound weight limits is a leasing liability in 2026. It signals to the pet-owner demographic — which represents the majority of renters — that your community isn't really for them.

A 2026 PetScreening survey found that 81% of rental operators report growth in pet ownership among their residents. Communities that respond by relaxing restrictions and adding amenities are seeing stronger lead volume and retention.

Consider replacing blanket breed restrictions with an individual pet screening process. Tools like PetScreening let you assess each animal on temperament and owner history rather than appearance. This approach reduces your liability, widens your applicant pool, and demonstrates that your community evaluates residents fairly. Pair it with a clear, reasonable pet fee structure — the national average pet rent sits around $35/month — and you've built a policy that's both protective and competitive.

Invest in the Right Pet Amenities

Not every amenity delivers equal return. Here's how the most common options stack up for Florida communities specifically:

Dog Parks and Green Space

Dog parks are the most cited amenity in pet-friendly listings, mentioned in over 58% of descriptions according to RentCafe. In Florida's year-round outdoor climate, a well-maintained fenced dog run is a strong baseline investment. Even a modest half-acre space with waste stations and benches signals that dogs are first-class residents, not guests being tolerated.

On-Site Pet Wash Stations

This is one of the fastest-growing amenities in multifamily housing — and one of the most practical. After a walk on a Florida beach or a muddy afternoon in the rain, the last thing a resident wants to do is wrestle a wet dog into a bathroom tub. On-site dog wash stations solve a real, recurring pain point. RentCafe found that pet washing stations now appear in nearly 12% of listing descriptions, and that number is climbing as residents increasingly expect them.

Operators like Sudspect Dog Wash install professional self-service dog wash stations directly at apartment communities — at no cost to the property — so residents get a premium amenity and management gets a meaningful differentiator on listings without a capital expense. For Florida properties that want a quick, high-visibility upgrade to their pet amenity package, it's worth exploring.

Pet Waste Management

Waste stations may not be glamorous, but neglecting them is one of the fastest ways to lose pet-owner residents — and to frustrate non-pet owners. Consistent, well-placed waste stations with reliable bag supplies show that management takes the shared-space experience seriously for everyone. According to the 2026 PetScreening survey, waste stations were the single most commonly offered pet amenity, cited by 45% of operators.

Make Pet-Friendliness Visible in Your Marketing

You can have excellent pet amenities and still lose applicants to a competitor if pet-friendliness isn't front and center in your marketing. Property managers often bury pet policies in FAQ pages. Leasing directors describe amenities generically on listing platforms. That's a missed opportunity.

A few high-impact adjustments:

  • Lead with pets in your listing titles and descriptions. "Pet-friendly apartments in [City], FL — dog wash station on-site" outperforms a generic headline for this demographic's search queries.
  • Add pet amenity photos to your photo gallery. Photos of your dog park, wash station, and waste bag dispensers tell a story that text can't replicate.
  • Highlight your pet policy on the community website. A dedicated "Pets Welcome" page — not just a buried FAQ item — signals genuine commitment.
  • Use social media to feature resident pets. A weekly Instagram feature of a resident's dog (with their permission) builds community, drives engagement, and reinforces your pet-friendly brand organically.

Build a Community Around Pet Ownership

Amenities draw people in. Community keeps them there. The properties with the strongest pet-owner retention aren't just the ones with the best dog parks — they're the ones where pet owners feel they belong.

Simple, low-cost programs make a difference here. A quarterly "Yappy Hour" mixer in a common area gives residents a reason to introduce themselves. A community bulletin board (physical or digital) where residents share pet care recommendations, lost pet alerts, or dog-walking schedule coordination builds the kind of social infrastructure that makes moving feel costly. When a resident's social network is embedded in a community, they stay.

You can also coordinate with local pet businesses — veterinary clinics, groomers, pet supply stores — to offer resident discounts. This creates value beyond the walls of your property and positions your community as an advocate for the pet-owner lifestyle, not just an accommodating landlord.

Measure What You're Getting Back

Pet amenities are an investment, and like any investment, they should be tracked. Measure the following on a quarterly basis:

  • Pet-owner lead volume — Are more applicants mentioning pets or filtering for pet-friendly units?
  • Lease renewal rates among pet owners — Are they renewing at higher rates than the overall property average?
  • Pet fee revenue — Is your pet rent and deposit structure generating meaningful incremental income?
  • Amenity usage — Are residents actually using the dog park and wash station? Low usage may indicate a placement or awareness problem.

Communities that treat pet amenities as a measurable business strategy — rather than a nice-to-have — tend to iterate faster and outperform competitors who set it and forget it.

The Bottom Line for Florida Property Managers

The renter demographic is shifting, and pet ownership is one of the clearest signals of where it's going. In a state like Florida — with year-round outdoor weather, a transient population, and a highly competitive multifamily market — the communities that win the pet-owner renter are going to win on occupancy, on lease renewal rates, and on resident satisfaction scores.

The good news: you don't have to overhaul your entire property to get there. Updating your pet policy, adding one or two high-visibility amenities, and making pet-friendliness prominent in your marketing can meaningfully shift your leasing pipeline within a single quarter.
