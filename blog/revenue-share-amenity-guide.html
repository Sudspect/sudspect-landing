What Is a Revenue Share Amenity Program? A Guide for Property Managers

Revenue share amenity programs are one of the fastest-growing NOI strategies in multifamily. Here's exactly how they work, what the property gets, and what to watch out for before you sign.

Property managers across Florida are under constant pressure to grow net operating income without raising rents or taking on capital projects. Revenue share amenity programs have emerged as one of the most practical answers to that challenge — but the term gets used loosely, and not every program is structured the same way.

This guide breaks down exactly how revenue share amenity programs work, what a property should expect from a legitimate partner, and the questions you need to ask before committing your community to any agreement.

The Basic Concept: You Supply the Space, They Supply Everything Else

A revenue share amenity program is a placement arrangement between a property and a vendor who installs and operates a revenue-generating amenity on-site. The core structure is simple:

  1. The vendor installs the amenity at no cost to the property — no equipment purchase, no installation fee.
  2. The vendor owns and operates the amenity — handling all maintenance, restocking, repairs, and customer service.
  3. Residents pay to use it — the amenity is a paid service, not a free perk.
  4. The property earns a percentage of every transaction — automatically, without any operational involvement from staff.

The property contributes square footage or placement space. The vendor contributes everything else. The resulting income is split according to the terms in the agreement.

Why This Model Exists — and Why It's Growing

Revenue share programs exist because both sides benefit in ways they couldn't achieve alone. Vendors get distribution — access to captive, high-density audiences of residents who need or want the service. Properties get amenity upgrades and passive income without capital expenditure or operational burden.

The model has accelerated in multifamily because the economics of pet ownership have shifted dramatically. More than 70% of U.S. renters now own pets, and that figure is even higher among younger renters in Florida's major metros. Amenities that serve pet owners — dog wash stations, pet supply vending, waste management systems — now generate meaningful usage volumes at properties with 150 or more units.

At the same time, property management companies are under pressure to grow NOI without rent increases. Revenue share placements are one of the few strategies that add income immediately with no capital requirement, no staffing addition, and no disruption to existing operations.

What a Legitimate Revenue Share Agreement Covers

Not all programs are equally structured. A legitimate, property-friendly revenue share agreement should clearly specify the following:

Ownership and liability

The vendor should own the equipment. This means the property has no liability for the machine — no insurance requirement, no repair obligation, no write-off if it breaks. Confirm this in writing before signing anything.

Maintenance responsibility

All maintenance, repairs, and restocking should fall entirely on the vendor. The property's staff should never be expected to troubleshoot, clean, or restock the amenity. Ask what the vendor's response time is for out-of-service machines — anything longer than 48 hours is a red flag for a high-use amenity.

Revenue tracking and payment terms

You should receive transparent, automated reporting on every transaction — not a monthly PDF that you have to trust without verification. Understand when and how you get paid (monthly is standard), and whether the payment method is automatic or requires invoicing.

Utility costs

Some vendors pass water and electricity costs back to the property. Others absorb them entirely. This is a significant variable — a dog wash station uses water at scale, and if that cost lands on your utility bill, it affects your actual net revenue. Clarify this before signing.

Minimum term and exit provisions

Understand how long you're committing and what happens if performance doesn't meet expectations. A reputable vendor will offer reasonable termination terms if the amenity underperforms, rather than locking a property into a multi-year commitment regardless of results.

The Math: What Does a Property Actually Earn?

Revenue share percentages vary by vendor and amenity type, but dog wash stations typically generate the following at a well-promoted 200-unit property:

  • Average wash price: $24.99–$39.99 per session
  • Estimated monthly usage at a 200-unit property: 40–80 washes/month (20–40% pet adoption rate, 1–2 washes/month per pet)
  • Gross monthly revenue: $1,120–$2,800
  • Property's share (at a typical 40–50% split): $448–$1,400/month

The range is wide because usage is directly tied to resident awareness. Properties that actively promote the amenity — newsletter mentions, signage near dog walk areas, inclusion in move-in packets — consistently see usage at the higher end of the range. Properties that install and say nothing see usage at the lower end.

This is the key insight: in a revenue share model, the property is an active partner in its own income. The more residents know the amenity exists, the more money the property makes.

Revenue Share vs. Buying the Equipment Outright

Some properties consider purchasing and owning a dog wash station directly. Here's a quick comparison:

Factor Revenue Share Property-Owned
Upfront cost $0 $8,000–$15,000+
Maintenance responsibility Vendor Property
Equipment liability Vendor Property
Revenue potential 40–50% of gross 100% (after costs)
Staff time required None Moderate (supply restocking, repairs)
Time to first revenue Weeks Months (procurement + installation)

For most property managers, the revenue share model wins on every dimension except total revenue ceiling — and even that gap closes quickly when you account for water costs, maintenance labor, and the time value of capital.

Questions to Ask Any Revenue Share Partner Before Signing

  1. Do you own the equipment, and does your insurance cover it on my property?
  2. What is your response time if the machine goes out of service?
  3. Are water and electricity costs included, or billed to the property?
  4. How is revenue tracked — can I see real-time transaction data?
  5. What is the minimum contract term, and what are the early termination terms?
  6. What does a typical property at my size earn per month after 90 days?

A partner with nothing to hide will answer all of these directly. Vague answers on maintenance response time, utility costs, or contract exit terms are red flags worth pressing on.

Is Your Property a Good Fit?

Revenue share dog wash programs work best at properties with 150 or more units, a meaningful pet-owner population, and at least one outdoor common area where a kiosk can be conveniently placed. Florida's year-round climate makes usage consistent across all 12 months — unlike northern markets where winter dramatically reduces outdoor pet activity.
